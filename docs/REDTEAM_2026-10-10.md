# Red-team review, 2026-10-10

Adversarial review of the three-sleeve book ([SLEEVES.md](SLEEVES.md)) before deployment, using the red-team process from the team's UNSW Battlecode 2026 project (its CLAUDE.md sections 21-22): for every part of the system, ask how it could be made to lose, record the weakness, how it is exploited, whether it is local or architectural, its importance, and the fix and its tradeoffs, and try to falsify assumptions rather than confirm them.

**How it ran.** Four independent red teams, each followed by a separate verifier told to refute every finding, then one fixer for confirmed findings, which had to reproduce each as a failing test before fixing it:
- *Market*: synthetic scenarios (regime whipsaw, momentum crash, alt dump, small-cap pump and dump, slow bleed, single-coin outlier, late regime turn) through the backtest and the live engine.
- *Execution*: Roostoo and Binance failure modes against the live engine and broker, with mocked APIs.
- *Scoring*: how the composite responds to the remaining days from about $97.5k, under the scorer's defaults and alternative annualization and drawdown conventions (Monte Carlo).
- *Evidence*: every parameter checked against the team's committed research on branches `live-readiness` and `midcap-model-research`.

**Limits.** The container had no network access to Binance, Roostoo or any market data. Market and scoring results are synthetic and say how the design behaves, not how it will perform; only the evidence team used real (committed) backtest numbers. Decisions that need real prices are left to `scripts/sleeves_backtest.py` on the EC2 instance, with the rules in SLEEVES.md.

**Outcome.** 28 findings, 27 confirmed by the verifiers (most downgraded in importance), 1 uncertain. Code fixes: 7 findings with regression tests (156 tests pass). Strategy parameters were not changed on synthetic evidence; three open questions (the 3% de-risk stage, the mid regime gate, 17 vs 22 mid coins) get a real-data comparison and a decision rule.
